<p>git.wtf is a set of static pages. Nothing on it asks you to sign up or log in, and there are no ads. We do count visits, with Google Analytics.</p>
<h2>What gets recorded</h2>
<p>The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub keeps the usual server logs, your IP address among them, under <a href="https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement">its own privacy statement</a>. We do not see those logs.</p>
<h2>Analytics</h2>
<p>Google Analytics, loaded through Google Tag Manager, tells us which pages are read, where visitors come from and roughly where in the world they are. It sets cookies to tell a returning visitor from a new one. We see totals, not people. Google handles that data under <a href="https://policies.google.com/privacy">its privacy policy</a>, and you can turn it off with your browser's tracking protection or <a href="https://tools.google.com/dlpage/gaoptout">Google's opt-out add-on</a>.</p>
<h2>What stays in your browser</h2>
<p>If you turn the sound on or off in an episode, your browser remembers that choice for next time. It stays on your device and never reaches us.</p>
<h2>Sharing and links out</h2>
<p>The share buttons open X, LinkedIn or your device's share sheet with the link filled in. Links to other sites carry ref=git.wtf so those sites can tell where the visit came from. Once you are there, their own policies apply.</p>
<h2>Confessions</h2>
<p>Confessions go in as issues on the site's public GitHub repository, so anything you write there is public. Leave out keys, passwords and other people's names. GitHub's terms apply to what you post there.</p>
<h2>Changes</h2>
<p>If any of this changes, this page changes with it. Last updated 8 November 2026.</p>
